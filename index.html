Act One

The Rise

Two decades of near-unbroken growth. A pandemic that erased the industry in a single year. Then a record-breaking comeback that nobody predicted.

In 2024, Australians attended live concerts more than at any other point in history — 31.4 million tickets sold and $3.41 billion in revenue. Both figures shatter every previous record.

Act Two

The Map

Live music is geographically concentrated. Where you live determines how much live music you can access — and that gap is widening.

New South Wales and Victoria together account for 64% of all live music revenue and 61% of attendance — while housing just 52% of Australia's population. The Northern Territory holds two festivals. New South Wales holds 149.

Chart 05  ·  Geographic Map (Mercator projection)  ·  Sources: Live Performance Australia; Creative Australia Soundcheck 2024
Where the Music Lives

What: A proportional symbol map. Circles are positioned by state capital coordinates; their size and colour both encode total revenue — larger and brighter means more revenue. Diamond markers show 39 major festivals, colour-coded by genre. Why: The spatial encoding immediately reveals the east-coast dominance of NSW and VIC, and the near-absence of festivals in central and northern Australia.

Act Four

The Crisis

Record crowds. Record revenue. And yet Australian music festivals are collapsing at an alarming rate. The paradox at the heart of the live music scene has never been starker.

In 2024 alone, Splendour in the Grass, Groovin the Moo, Spilt Milk, and Lost Paradise were all cancelled. Bluesfest — a 35-year institution — announced 2025 as its final edition. Stadium tours are booming. The grassroots festivals that built Australian music culture are dying.
